How to practise a mock interview at home

By InterviewCrack · Updated 17 October 2026

To practise a mock interview at home, choose a target role, prepare five relevant questions, answer them aloud without notes, and review one specific improvement before repeating the session.

Plan a 25-minute practice session

  1. Minutes 0–5: choose your target. Read the job description and list three skills it asks for. Choose an introduction question, two role-specific questions, one behavioural question, and one question about your motivation.
  2. Minutes 5–15: answer aloud. Spend roughly two minutes per answer as a practice constraint, not a rule for real interviews. Use a timer. If you lose your place, pause and restart the sentence instead of reading a script.
  3. Minutes 15–20: review. Listen to a recording if you chose to make one, or write down what you remember. Identify where you stopped answering the question or made a claim without an example.
  4. Minutes 20–25: repeat one answer. Keep the same question. Change one thing: a clearer opening, a stronger example, or a shorter conclusion.

Which questions should I start with?

  • Tell me about yourself and how your experience connects to this role.
  • Walk me through a project where you used a skill this job needs.
  • How would you investigate a problem you have not seen before?
  • Tell me about a time a plan failed. What did you do next?
  • Why does this role interest you, and what would you like to learn?

How do I evaluate an answer?

Use three checks: relevance (did I answer the question?), evidence (did I explain what I personally did?), and clarity (could a listener follow the sequence?). Mark each as clear, partial, or missing. This is a self-review aid, not a prediction of a hiring decision.

Example: improve a vague project answer

Vague: “I worked on the frontend and improved the user experience.”

More specific: “I owned the checkout form. Users were losing their entries after a validation error, so I preserved their input and moved error messages beside the relevant fields. I checked the revised flow with our tester before release.”

This is an illustrative example. Use your actual responsibilities and results; do not invent metrics.
